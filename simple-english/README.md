# Simple English

A practical English project for people who already know some English but still have gaps in the basics. Its purpose is to help them express their own ideas more clearly and confidently, especially when writing or publishing in English.

This is about practice, not certificates. It does not aim to reduce the learner to a fixed level or make every text use only the easiest words. It helps the learner say what they mean in natural English, then use useful words and phrases again until they become familiar.

## Two connected goals

- **Use English:** improve messages, posts, presentations, and other writing while keeping the learner's meaning, voice, and intended tone.
- **Grow English:** notice useful gaps in everyday vocabulary, grammar, and common phrases, then practise a few relevant items at a time.

When improving a text, the skill should show a natural version and briefly explain useful changes. It should teach reusable phrases and patterns, not just replace the text without explanation. English should sound clear and natural, not childish or artificially restricted.

Speaking practice can use short prompts, example answers, and natural phrases that the learner can reuse. The goal is to become more comfortable expressing real thoughts in English, including in public.

## Vocabulary foundation

The first ready-to-use list is the 2,809-word New General Service List (NGSL 1.2). It is divided into three project stages:

1. Ranks 1–850 — foundation.
2. Ranks 851–1,500 — expansion.
3. Ranks 1,501–2,809 — wider core.

These are project learning bands, not official CEFR levels or a required order for lessons. The list is frequency-ranked; practical lessons should focus on words and phrases the learner needs, check what they already know, and use examples from real communication.

Simple English Wikipedia is a reference for clarity and direct sentence structure. It is not a strict limit for this project. The goal is natural English that a confident non-native speaker can use and publish.

## Privacy boundary

This public folder is for general project materials and reusable skill content. Personal vocabulary progress, notes, and preferences belong in private storage.
